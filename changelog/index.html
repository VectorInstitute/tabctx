    [0.21.0] - 2026-11-13¶

    Added¶

    • Two new tasks, survival and causal, with SurvivalPFN and CausalPFN (TABCTX_BACKEND=...,survivalpfn,causalpfn). Both models are about 20M parameters (75 MB of weights) and fit beside the others on one GPU.
    • Survival (survivalpfn-v0.1, SurvivalPFN, Apache-2.0): time to an event with right censoring. A fit sends each row's time as train_y and its event flag as train_event (or a file's event_column); a predict returns each row's median event time, quantiles of the event time, with times the probability of no event by each (survival, survival_times), and with rmst_horizon the restricted mean survival time up to it (rmst). Nothing is estimated past the longest training time (the fit response's summary.max_time; that row is always kept in the context): those values, rmst included, are null (with heavy censoring the model put near-zero survival there, against a true 0.74).
    • Causal (causalpfn-v0, CausalPFN, Apache-2.0): a binary treatment's effect from observational data. A fit sends each row's outcome and its treatment flag (train_treatment / treatment_column); a predict returns each row's conditional average treatment effect, with potential_outcomes the expected outcome if not treated and if treated, and quantiles of a treated minus an untreated outcome (CausalPFN's interval method). The quantiles are calibrated as CausalPFN's calibrate=True does (a temperature by 3-fold cross-validation, computed exactly rather than from samples, at a context's first predict that asks for quantiles, so fits that never ask pay nothing): on simulated data a 90% range went from 5x the outcome noise's spread to its spread. They read each row's two distributions at fixed levels instead of sampling them, so they repeat exactly. Missing values are filled with the training medians. The effect matches CausalPFN's own estimator when the table fits the context; for a larger table, CausalPFN picks a context per group of test rows, which made a row's answer depend on the rest of its request, so tabctx uses one subsampled context instead (below).
    • One context per fit. Each model keeps at most 4,096 training rows as its context (TABCTX_SURVIVALPFN_CONTEXT_SIZE, TABCTX_CAUSALPFN_CONTEXT_SIZE; a larger table is subsampled at fit, seeded, stratified on both targets: the share of events or of each arm, and the spread of times or outcomes within it), and test rows attend only to it, so a row's answer is the same alone, merged with other requests or split into chunks. The models take any number of rows; a larger context costs predict time (roughly with its square: 1,000 survival rows on an A40 took 0.12 s at 4,096 and 50 s at 100,000) and, on four of SurvivalPFN's benchmark datasets larger than 4,096 rows (up to 36,695: hdfail, prostateSurvival, Dialysis, dataDIVAT1), added at most 0.01 C-index.
    • Checked against SurvivalPFN's published benchmark (tests/integration/test_survivalpfn_reference.py, uv sync --group survivalpfn --group survivalpfn-reference): on the authors' own splits of six small public datasets (veteran, mgus, whas500, stagec, Melanoma, zinc), SurvivalPFN's medians reproduce the published C-index, tabctx's medians equal them wherever given, and ranking rows by rmst up to the longest training time scores within 0.01 of it (0.015 tolerance in the test). With heavy censoring many medians are past the follow-up (null; zinc, 81% censored: C-index 0.621 from the medians, 0.784 from rmst), so rmst is the way to rank rows (a higher rmst is a lower risk). SurvivalPFN is not calibrated: its authors found CausalPFN's temperature calibration makes it worse.
    • What a predict returns (tabctx.outputs): beyond the predictions, quantiles, times, rmst_horizon and potential_outcomes, each for the tasks whose models give it, checked before any work (a 422 otherwise, and for return_proba on a survival or causal context). One validated value carries them through the engine, devices, worker processes and the coalescer, so adding an output is a field there and its backends.
    • A survival or causal fit's response has a summary: max_time, events and censored, or treated and control.
    • Each fit's task picks its model: a backend lists its tasks, GET /v1/models shows them with each model's outputs (now with a response schema), and a fit that names no model goes to the default if it fits the task, else the first that does. A model that doesn't fit the task is a 422 naming one that does. An event or treatment column implies its task.
    • Both networks load once per device, as tensors only (torch.load(weights_only=True); the models' own loaders unpickle arbitrary objects), from pinned Hugging Face revisions; the spill tier stores a context without its network.
    • On a CPU, on simulated data (1,500 training rows, 500 held out): SurvivalPFN's medians had a C-index of 0.922 against the observed times (0.942 for the true risk itself); CausalPFN's effects correlated 0.998 with the true ones. On an A40, both served over HTTP with a peak of 1.1 GB, and 8 concurrent users got the same answers as sequential requests. Tables wider than 99 features are reduced to 99 by truncated SVD (as the models' packages do) and lose accuracy: on a 150-column simulation, C-index 0.67 against 0.72 at 99 columns.
    • pip install "tabctx[causalpfn]". SurvivalPFN is not on PyPI, so it is a uv dependency group (uv sync --group survivalpfn), pinned to a commit; the image includes both.
    • Event and treatment columns in files may be 0/1, true/false or yes/no; with no target named, the target is the last column that isn't the flag.
    • The client: fit(..., event=) / treatment= (sending the task they imply, so a server without these tasks refuses the fit rather than fitting a regression), fit_file and fit_uploaded with event_column / treatment_column, predict(..., times=, rmst_horizon=, potential_outcomes=), and tabular_models(task) / default_model(task); the scikit-learn estimators and the MCP tools only pick models that fit their task.

    [0.20.0] - 2026-11-10¶

    Added¶

    • An insurance scenario for the agent demo. A coding agent gets two messy raw exports from a made-up insurer's policy system (synthetic data: dates in four formats, a province spelled five ways, rows exported twice, test policies, a totals row), writes and runs a cleaning script, then finds which policyholders will cancel at renewal, why, and which retention offers move their risk, and writes a report for the head of retention. DEMO_SCENARIO=insurance picks it; readmission stays the default and the hosted demo. Scenarios are now folders in examples/agent_demo/scenarios/ (task, prompt, data), found through scenario.py; a task with a "clean" step gives each agent its shell (Claude Code's only inside its sandbox, which keeps it out of the answers). A Claude Code run against TabICL on a laptop took about five minutes.

    Fixed¶

    • A long table name no longer breaks every fit when the disk tier is on. The disk tier named its files with the hex of the full id (tenant plus the caller's name), which doubles its length, so a name over about 80 characters passed the 255-byte file name limit and every fit of it failed with a 500 (OSError: File name too long, raised while dropping a stale spilled copy). On prod a lab's descriptive names failed all morning. Ids whose hex name would pass the limit are now named by their SHA-256, with the id kept in the metadata file; every id that had a working hex name keeps it, so files spilled before this change still load. Spill files are written whole or not at all (a temporary file, then a rename), and a hashed pair whose metadata can't be read is removed at startup instead of holding disk forever.
    • A saturated replica no longer reads as down to a gateway. The standalone server's request limiter no longer sheds GET /v1/models or /-/healthz (Ray Serve's health path, a 404 here), which a gateway's readiness probe asks, and /v1/models is answered on the event loop rather than behind fits and predicts in the threadpool.
    • A predict reported cache_outcome: hit when the table was restored from disk while its rows were being read, as on a replica after a restart (its schema not yet recorded); the restore is now counted wherever it happens.

    Changed¶

    • cache_outcome is required in the predict response's schema (the server always sent it; generated clients now see it as always present). The coalescing batcher closes a batch and releases everyone in it even if its window's wait fails.

    [0.19.0] - 2026-11-05¶

    Fixed¶

    • A 422 for a non-finite number in a request (Infinity) no longer becomes a 500: the error echoes the value as text.
    • /readyz no longer waits behind long fits (#51). It read GPU memory through torch's allocator (which waits for a fit freeing memory), asked each GPU worker for a report (up to 30 s behind its queued calls), took the host-memory guard's cross-process lock and walked the spill directory; on prod, readiness timed out during large fits, so a busy replica looked hung, and with the Helm chart's single replica that would take it out of its Service. Now every one of those is measured on its own thread every 5 s (worker processes send theirs), and /readyz reads the latest results and the guard's counters without a lock. It reports how old the device measurements are (device_report_age_s), and fails only for a dead worker or one silent for 10 minutes. Polled every 0.2 s during a 49 s TabICL fit on a CPU: median 1 ms, at most 353 ms. /v1/tabctx/limits reads the cache's counters without its lock too. A worker that never reports goes stale from its start; a spill tier that can't be read is shown as an error rather than a stuck worker. Not yet verified on a GPU: on a single-GPU replica serving in-process, whether torch releases the interpreter lock while its measuring call waits on the allocator; replicas with GPU worker processes measure in those processes, out of /readyz's way.

    Added¶

    • Predict responses and PredictResult expose cache_outcome: hit for a resident context or restored after disk spillover. Coalesced requests share their batch's outcome; older deployments leave the client field as None. (#112; the project's first outside contribution, from @sunxiuguo in #115.)
    • Retention limits (#103). TABCTX_MAX_CONTEXT_AGE_S caps how long any fitted table is kept after its fit, including tables fit before it was set (one spilled by an older tabctx, with no recorded fit time, counts as expired); a fit can ask for less with ttl_s, counted from when the request arrives (HTTP; TabctxClient.fit, fit_file and fit_uploaded; the scikit-learn estimators, which apply it again on every refit). At that time the context is deleted from device memory, host memory, the disk tier and the server's schema cache, whether or not anyone uses it again: a predict after it is a 404, and each engine sweeps expired contexts every 30 s (in worker processes too), re-checking each under the cache lock so a refit of the same id is never deleted. Fit responses and GET /v1/tabctx/datasets show expires_at (the listing leaves out expired tables); /v1/tabctx/limits shows the server's limit; the Helm chart sets it with maxContextAgeS.
    • "Why a server", a docs page for people arriving from the models' own packages: what changes when a model serves many tables, users and programs (repeat predicts, many tables per GPU, out-of-memory, keeping a fit, uncertainty, licensing), what tabctx does at each step, and when not to use it. Each need links to a thread where users of these models raised it.
    • TabDPT, a third model (#102): TABCTX_BACKEND=tabdpt (or tabicl,tabdpt) serves Layer 6's TabDPT as tabdpt-1.3. Its code and weights are Apache-2.0, so with TabICLv2 there are now two models a business can serve without a separate license. Install with pip install "tabctx[tabdpt]"; the container image includes it.
    • On credit-g (the quickstart's split) it scored 0.768 against TabICL's 0.772; on California housing, R² 0.89, with its 90% interval holding 88.4% of held-out prices (python examples/prediction_intervals.py --model tabdpt-1.3), on a CPU. Classification, regression, prediction intervals, list and delete, and the spill tier all work as for the other models.
    • TabDPT keeps no encoding of the training table: its fit stores the table, and each predict samples a context of TABCTX_TABDPT_CONTEXT_SIZE rows (2,048) from it, with TABCTX_TABDPT_ENSEMBLES (4) ensemble members for both tasks. Left unbounded, as TabDPT's default is, a predict's cost grows with the square of the table (8,000 rows: 45 s on a CPU, against 4 s). tabctx adds serving: one endpoint, admission control, tenancy.
    • One network per device, shared by every context and both tasks; the spill tier stores a context without it, tagged with the checkpoint version.
    • Flash attention on every CUDA device (TabDPT enables it only for the device name "cuda", so a multi-GPU replica's "cuda:1" ran without it).
    • Predicts use a fixed seed, so the same request gives the same answer; the mean is the same with or without quantiles.
    • Numeric labels keep numeric order (1, 2, 10), as with the other models. Bad test rows (an infinite value) are a 422.
    • A Helm chart (#105) in deploy/helm/tabctx, with a "Deploy on Kubernetes" guide. One tabctx pod with its GPUs:
    • /livez (startup, liveness) and /readyz (readiness) probes, with the startup budget covering the first weights download and kernel compilation;
    • a persistent volume for weights and compiled kernels, and an opt-in one for the disk tier (both kept on uninstall, or bring your own claim);
    • a TabPFN token from an existing Secret;
    • the Pod Security "restricted" profile (non-root, no capabilities, RuntimeDefault seccomp);
    • a 300 s shutdown grace for in-flight fits;
    • the settings as values, plus extraEnv (which replaces a variable the chart sets rather than duplicating it) and extra volumes.
    • Rendering refuses what would fail at runtime: tabpfn without a token, replicaCount > 1 with ReadWriteOnce volumes, sizes under 1 GiB.
    • CI lints it and checks those refusals; the chart's version must match the package's (a unit test). Release tags push it to oci://ghcr.io/vectorinstitute/charts/tabctx.
    • Installed on a local kind cluster (CPU, fake model): fit, predict, list and the tenant check worked through the Service, and the non-root user could write its volumes.
    • A documentation site at https://vectorinstitute.github.io/tabctx/ (#9), built with MkDocs Material and published from main by .github/workflows/docs.yml. Pull requests build it with --strict, so broken links and missing API symbols fail. It has:
    • a landing page;
    • a quickstart, and guides for Python and scikit-learn and for the HTTP API;
    • the serving, agents, architecture and benchmark docs;
    • a reference generated from the code: the Python API from docstrings, the HTTP API from the service's own OpenAPI spec (rendered with Redoc), and configuration;
    • a guide to adding a model.
    • The OpenAPI spec names the service tabctx at its real version, groups routes (contexts, deployment, health, legacy) and gives each a summary.
    • Prediction intervals for regression. A predict can ask for quantiles (levels in (0, 1), e.g. [0.05, 0.5, 0.95] for the median and a 90% interval), and each test row gets the predicted distribution's value at each level. TabICL and TabPFN both predict a distribution over the target, so the quantiles come from the same forward pass as the mean: no extra GPU work. examples/prediction_intervals.py measures how well calibrated they are: on California housing (TabICL, 2,250 training and 750 held-out rows) the 90% interval held 86.5% of the prices, a little narrower than nominal.
    • HTTP: quantiles in POST /v1/tabctx/predict, returned as quantiles and quantile_levels. JSON takes a list; a multipart form takes one field, comma- or space-separated or a JSON array (a repeated field is refused, as for every form field). Bad levels are a 422, as are quantiles for a classification context (it returns probabilities) or from a model that predicts only a point. The legacy /v1/tabicl/predict doesn't take them.
    • Python: TabctxRegressor.predict_quantiles(X, quantiles=(0.05, 0.5, 0.95)) returns an (n_samples, n_levels) array (bad levels raise ValueError). TabctxClient.predict(..., quantiles=...) and predict_file return PredictResult.quantiles and quantile_levels, check the levels before sending, and raise if the deployment predates quantiles instead of silently returning none.
    • Engine and backends: quantiles= on predict throughout; the backend protocol documents it, and a backend without it still serves every other predict. Concurrent predicts are only coalesced with others asking for the same levels.
    • List and delete contexts (#103, part 1). GET /v1/tabctx/datasets lists the caller's contexts, resident or spilled: id, model, task, shape, size, location, GPU and fit time (metadata only). DELETE /v1/tabctx/datasets/{dataset_id} removes one from device memory, host memory and the disk tier on every GPU, or answers 404 if there is none. Both are scoped by tenant like every /v1/tabctx route. See "Listing and deleting contexts" in docs/serving.md.
    • A delete is atomic with respect to spills and restores: one in progress lands first, so no copy reappears afterwards. If it doesn't land in time, the delete answers 503 having deleted nothing.
    • On a multi-GPU replica the delete asks every GPU at once, not just the ones the residency snapshot lists. It never reports success while a copy may survive: while a GPU worker is down (its spilled copies would come back with it), list and delete answer 503 before touching anything, and a GPU failing mid-delete makes it 503 too. The client retries, and a retry that finds the context gone counts as deleted.
    • Worker processes answer listings and deletes from a small pool of their own, so they wait neither behind queued fits nor in front of /readyz.
    • Client: TabctxClient.datasets() and TabctxClient.delete(dataset_id).
    • Agents: a delete_model tool (and tabctx-mcp delete <name>) removes a model's context on the server and its saved files, which include its training table. A context another saved model shares (same table, model and task) is kept, and a model whose signature doesn't verify still loses its files without the server being asked. Saved models now record their context id.
    • Spilled contexts now record their fit time; ones spilled by older versions list it as null.
    • A new error, CacheUnavailableError (503), for "can't list or delete correctly right now".

    Fixed¶

    • A caller without a tenant id could reach a tenant's context by naming its scoped id (t:<tenant>:<id>) on deployments that don't require tenants: predict on it, overwrite it, and now delete it. Ids starting with t: are now refused (422) for callers without a tenant id.
    • TabctxClient's GET requests send the tenant header, as its POSTs always did, and requests without a body no longer send a Content-Type.
    • TabctxClient raised UploadNotFoundError for any 404 whose message contained "upload", including a missing dataset with "upload" in its id. It now matches only the upload store's own message.

    Changed¶

    • The unit tests run in parallel (pytest -n auto, with pytest-xdist in the dev dependencies), as CI now does.

    [0.18.0] - 2026-11-04¶

    Changed¶

    • A shorter README with a logo and a headline figure. The front page now has the pitch, a fit-vs-predict chart from a saved A40 sweep (benchmarks/plot_readme_figure.py draws it from benchmarks/baselines/sweep-a40x4-tabicl-v2.json), a quickstart that runs on a laptop with no API key (checked against a fresh install of 0.17.1 from PyPI), the agent tools, a comparison with in-process models and hosted APIs, and each model's weights license. The detail moved, unchanged, into docs/serving.md, docs/how-it-works.md, docs/benchmarks.md and docs/coding-agents.md. Images use absolute URLs so the PyPI page shows them too.

    Fixed¶

    • The estimators and tabctx-mcp use the deployment's own default model when none is named, instead of always tabpfn-3.5. Before, TabctxClassifier(), TabctxRegressor() and the agent tools' fit_model/explain failed with a 422 on any deployment that doesn't serve TabPFN, and evaluate reported an error for TabPFN there.
    • model=None (the default) is resolved at fit time from GET /v1/models, and the estimators record the answer as model_. The context id is computed from that resolved model, so naming the default explicitly reuses the same cached context.
    • tabctx's own listing marks its default. A gateway lists its whole catalog with no mark: Vector's lists 65 LLMs beside tabicl-v2 and tabpfn-3.5. There, the client narrows the list to the tabular models it knows (tabctx.client.KNOWN_MODELS) and prefers tabpfn-3.5, as before.
    • evaluate with no models runs every tabular model served, and never a gateway's LLMs.
    • New: TabctxClient.tabular_models() and TabctxClient.default_model().
    • GET requests now retry on 503, like fit and predict.
    • The agent tools check their arguments and read the table before asking the server.
    • pip install "tabctx[client]" installs pandas. The estimators are built for DataFrames, and scikit-learn's loaders (fetch_openml(as_frame=True)) need pandas, so the documented quickstart failed on a clean install.
    • from tabctx import TabctxClient works (also PredictResult). Before, the client could only be imported from tabctx.client.

    Removed¶

    • tabctx.estimators.DEFAULT_MODEL and tabctx.mcp_tools.tools.MODELS: the default now comes from the deployment. tabctx.client.KNOWN_MODELS lists the tabular model ids the client recognises in a gateway's catalog.

    [0.17.0] - 2026-10-31¶

    Added¶

    • A Ray-free standalone host: python -m tabctx.serve (the new standalone extra: [serve] minus ray) serves the same routes as the Ray Serve deployment from one plain uvicorn process, for platforms that don't run Ray -- e.g. a Kubernetes Deployment behind a gateway. The service moved to serve/service.py, host-agnostic and importable without ray (route handlers unchanged; ROUTE_SPECS captures the routes at import time so serve/standalone.py's make_app() can rebind them to a plain instance); serve/app.py keeps its import path and is now only the Ray host (the deployment settings and the consistent-hash affinity router). Serve-isms get standalone replacements: GET /livez answers 503 exactly when check_health would fail the replica (a dead per-GPU worker -- /readyz is the wrong liveness probe, it also 503s on transient pressure), and a request limiter replicates max_ongoing_requests/max_queued_requests shedding (TABCTX_MAX_ONGOING_REQUESTS/TABCTX_MAX_QUEUED_REQUESTS, defaults 8/16, probes exempt). One replica only: nothing outside Ray routes a dataset's predicts back to the replica that fit it. Unit-tested over httpx's ASGI transport in the ray-less unit environment (fit/predict inline and multipart, tenancy, 404/422, limiter, livez); the integration suite still proves serve/app.py on a live Ray cluster.
    • A serving image (Dockerfile, pushed to ghcr.io/vectorinstitute/tabctx:<version> by .github/workflows/docker_image.yml on each release tag): python:3.12-slim + a pinned torch (PyPI wheels bundle the CUDA runtime; the host supplies only the driver) + tabctx[standalone,tabicl,tabpfn], entrypoint python -m tabctx.serve --host 0.0.0.0, non-root. Weights are mounted, not baked. CI smoke-tests the image against the fake backend (imports, readyz/livez, tenant-gated fit and predict); GPU validation stays with the deploying platform's rehearsal. Every dependency except torch's own stack is installed at its uv.lock version (CI checks it), so a PR's smoke build and the release ship the same model libraries; the image carries gcc so torch.compile can build Triton's C helper instead of silently running eager kernels (about 1.3x slower).

Each backend estimates a call's host use (TabICL ~5 KB per cell when it offloads, else nothing; TabPFN ~0.6 KB per cell), and a guard admits calls against one budget shared by every worker process on the node: first come first served (a large fit can't be starved by modest ones), at most slots offloading calls at once (each budgets 1/slots of free host memory for its staging), calls under 1 GiB never wait. The budget is the container's memory limit x 0.85 minus its working set (every process in the pod) and an 8 GiB reserve, re-measured once the per-GPU workers have warmed up. A worker that dies holding host memory has it returned by its parent; a stuck accounting lock fails calls with HostMemoryUnavailableError (new, exported; HTTP 503, retryable) instead of hanging. /readyz gains host_memory (budget_bytes, reserved_bytes, calls_active, calls_offloading, calls_waiting, large_fit_slots).
    • TabICL fits prepare their CPU half before taking the device: preprocessing (~30-37% of a fit) overlaps another fit's GPU half -- back-to-back fits 17-26% faster on an A100. At most one fit per device prepares ahead, so queued fits don't each hold preprocessed copies in host memory.
    • Worker processes shut down together under one 10 s deadline (was up to 10 s each, past a pod's grace period).
    • tabicl's per-call log(n) host-to-device syncs are cached (bit-identical): 10k x 50 fits 1.98 -> 1.66 s on an A100.
    • The cache's spill reads and writes run outside its lock, so bookkeeping and light predicts never wait on multi-GB disk I/O.
    • TabPFN contexts share the model: ~42x less GPU memory per context. TabPFN builds a fresh model for every estimator unless its built-model cache is on, so every cached context held its own ~850 MB copy of the weights on the GPU (measured on an A100: 4 contexts of a 300x6 table took 3.4 GB). TabPFNBackend now enables that cache (TABPFN_MODEL_CACHE_SIZE, default 16 entries, keyed by device and task): one shared model per (device, task), about 1.7 GB fixed per GPU, and a context costs only its own state (~20 MB for that table). Interleaved contexts predict bit-identically to each context alone.
    • TabPFN spill blobs no longer contain the model. A spilled TabPFN payload was ~900 MB, mostly weights, and a restore built another private copy on the GPU. References to the shared model are now pickled as tokens and re-attached on load: a 2,000x20 context spills as 99 MB and restores with identical predictions and no extra GPU memory. Older blobs still load.
    • Weights load at replica start (warmup() on both backends, called by the factory) instead of inside the first tenant's fit, which reported the checkpoint as part of its context (a 200x6 TabPFN context was booked at 855 MB, TabICL's at 226 MB) and paid the load latency.

    Fixed¶

    • Fits OOMed on GPUs the books said had room (4x L4 rehearsal: a 250 x 1000 TabPFN fit beside 18 GB of contexts on a 22 GB card). Memory outside the cache's accounting grows after warmup (allocator fragmentation, compiled kernels' workspaces), so making room now also checks the device's real free memory (free plus torch's reusable cache) and evicts until both agree. A fit retrying after an OOM first frees contexts that were parked during its failed attempt.
    • Router 404 for a context being spilled (4xL4 rehearsal: a fit returned 200 and the next predict 404'd 27 ms later): a context moving between the GPU cache and the spill tier was listed by neither; it is now tracked in transit, and predict re-locates once after a miss.
    • TabICL shared-backbone cache was per-module, not per-call: interleaved calls could read another context's cache. It is now thread-local.
    • A device OOM while restoring a spilled context no longer deletes the spilled copy.
    • First fit on a GPU failed with "Invalid device argument": device-explicit memory-stat calls need CUDA initialized, which the no-argument forms used to do implicitly. Backends now initialize CUDA at construction.
    • Spill tier warm restart: the index rebuilt at startup was keyed by the hex-encoded file name instead of the dataset_id, so lookups, explicit deletes and disk-pressure cleanup all missed contexts a previous process had spilled (restores still worked because load() reads by path).
    • TabICL spill restore reloaded the checkpoint: tabicl's __setstate__ calls _load_model(), which loaded the full checkpoint (and a second backbone onto the GPU, unaccounted) on every restore before tabctx swapped in the shared one. Spilled TabICL payloads now carry a task tag and restore straight onto the shared backbone; untagged blobs from older versions still load.
    • /readyz and the legacy endpoint read GPU memory from torch's current device; they now read the replica's own device(s).
    • The TabICL and TabPFN backends measured memory on, and let library internals allocate on, torch's current CUDA device rather than their own. Harmless on one GPU; on cuda:i it would have charged every context to cuda:0. They now measure on their device and run inside its scope.

    Validated¶

    • Unit (multi-device placement, routing, spill restore, failures) and integration tests (the serve app on two fake devices through Ray). Concurrency tests force interleavings with gates and barriers rather than timing: a fit on an idle device completes while another device is mid-call, lookups and stats never wait on a busy device, concurrent same-id fits leave one copy, a predict during a re-fit is answered. Seven of them fail against the first version of this engine (found by review).
    • On a local fake 3-device replica with 5 concurrent users: with spill on, 0 failed predicts at ~100 predicts/s. Without spill, capacity eviction cost 12 predicts on three devices versus 115 on one.

    [0.9.0] - 2026-08-29¶

    Changed¶

    • Standard multi-model endpoint (chat-completions style). One deployment now serves several models over ONE shared context cache and GPU budget: TABCTX_BACKEND=tabicl,tabpfn (first listed = default), requests select a model via "model": "<exact id>" (tabicl-v2, tabpfn-3 -- named for the checkpoints actually loaded), GET /v1/models lists what's served. Single-model deployments are unchanged. Requires Python >= 3.12.
    • Admission control now bounds the right quantity, measured on real hardware (the former Priority-1 rewrite):
    • Backends report TWO measurements per fit: the transient PEAK allocation (what actually OOMs -- can exceed the resident context by an order of magnitude) and the post-fit RESIDENT size. Admission learns from peaks; cache accounting charges residents. (v0.4.0 conflated these -- admission was learning from residents.)
    • Admission is usage-aware: a fit's estimated peak must fit within 85% of device capacity MINUS what the cache already holds resident, so the same shape honestly stops being admissible as the replica fills and recovers after eviction. /v1/tabctx/limits reports the live boundary.
    • AdaptiveMemoryEstimator ships with a factory-preloaded calibration grid (memory/calibration_tabicl_a100.py, generated by benchmarks/calibrate_memory.py + gen_calibration_data.py from an A100-40GB sweep of TabICL with the kv cache on): measured peaks for shapes up to 100,000 rows x 200 features in "kv" mode and 500,000 rows in "repr" mode (whose contexts are ~10x smaller resident: 1.6GB vs 14.8GB at 100k x 100 -- the high-tenant-density option), with the real boundaries recorded: ~200k rows GPU-OOMs in kv mode, and ~10M-cell tables (200k x 100, 100k x 200) exhaust ~85GB of HOST RAM in the model's CPU-side pipeline -- pod memory limits are the backstop for that. Calibration points carry a 1.1x margin (direct measurements) vs 1.5x for runtime observations. Net effect, validated end to end on an A100: a 100,000 x 10 fit -- rejected by every prior version -- admits and completes in 17s, and fits beyond the measured boundary still 413 cleanly.
    • Disk spillover tier (TABCTX_SPILL_DIR, off by default): capacity-evicted contexts serialize to local disk and reload transparently on the next request -- no training data needed from the caller; bounded by its own LRU (TABCTX_SPILL_CAPACITY_BYTES), best-effort by contract, warm-restart capable. TabICL spills exclude the shared pretrained backbone. Validated under real pressure on an A100 (3 contexts / 39GB spilled, evicted context reloaded with correct predictions). Measured economics are in the module docstring: on slow disks a reload can lose to a re-fit -- enable it for fast NVMe, expensive fits, or callers who can't re-send data.
    • Evict-ahead-of-fit (found by the OOM it prevents, on a real A100): when a fit's estimated transient peak exceeds current headroom, cold contexts are evicted/spilled until it fits, instead of rejecting a fit that is safe once they step aside. Admission now runs under the cache lock (no two fits can pass on one snapshot), and the transient budget is capped at 85% of device capacity -- a fit admitted with ~400MB nominal slack at 90% still OOMed to allocator fragmentation.

    Added¶

    • tabctx.client.TabctxClient: pure-stdlib HTTP client that makes the serving contracts impossible to get wrong -- it sends x-session-id (== dataset_id, required for correct multi-replica routing) and x-tabctx-tenant-id automatically, maps HTTP errors back to the same tabctx exception types the in-process engine raises, and retries 503 backpressure with exponential backoff (safe by construction under strict affinity).     Performance¶

    • TabICL's kv-cache is now enabled by default (TabICLBackend(kv_cache="kv"), env-tunable via TABCTX_KV_CACHE=kv|repr|off). tabicl defaults it OFF, and without it every predict() silently re-encodes the ENTIRE training set through all three transformer stages -- i.e. the exact repeat cost tabctx exists to eliminate. Cannot exceed the memory budget: the engine still runs exactly one GPU call at a time and chunks oversized merged batches. /readyz reports coalescing counters.
